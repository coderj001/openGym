The best gym-person additions are not social features; they reduce friction while lifting:

 1. Previous performance beside each exercise
    “Last: 80 kg × 8 reps × 3” and a one-tap “copy last set.” Biggest in-workout win.

 2. Automatic rest timer after a completed work set
    Keep manual controls, but remove one repeated tap. Per-exercise overrides can come later.

 3. PR detection and celebration
    Detect best weight, reps-at-weight, estimated 1RM, and session volume—all computed locally.

 4. Plate calculator
    Enter target barbell weight; show plates per side using the user’s locally configured plates/bar weight.

 5. Edit completed workouts
    Lifters mistype sets. Allow correcting sets, notes, date, and body weight; progression already derives from history.

 6. Exercise-specific history
    From an exercise detail, show recent sessions, best set, e1RM trend, and prior notes—not just global charts.

 7. Workout/exercise notes
    Local-only notes such as “rack occupied,” “elbow sore,” or “use belt next time.” High practical value.

 8. Routine templates and duplicate routine
    Clone an existing routine for a cut, deload, home gym, or alternate equipment setup.

 9. Missed-workout handling
    “Move today’s Push to tomorrow” rather than silently losing schedule intent.

 10. Better volume feedback
        Weekly sets per muscle, compare against the user’s own recent baseline, and flag abrupt jumps—not generic fitness
     advice.

 11. Favorites, substitutions, and available-equipment profile
        Faster exercise picking when a gym is busy or home equipment is limited.

 12. True unit migration/display conversion
        The current kg/lb setting only changes labels. A lifter expects 83.5 kg to display as 184.1 lb; offer an explicit
     local conversion/migration action.

 I would prioritize previous performance + copy-last-set, automatic rest timer, PRs, and edit completed workout. Skip
 social feeds, cloud sync, AI coaching, and nutrition tracking until users prove they need them.
